I like turning ideas into things that actually work.

Much of my work sits in the awkward space between having a good idea and making it work in the real world.

I started out in computer science and software engineering, eventually completing a PhD in Computer Science, with research in the wider area of artificial intelligence.
Since then, my work has taken me through research on fair allocation and multi-agent systems, maritime decarbonisation, electric vehicle charging and citizen-centred AI.
Alongside the research itself, though, I was always interested in what it takes to turn research into something useful.

That might mean building a prototype, creating an industrial optimisation platform, shaping a project, leading and mentoring teams, bringing the right people together, finding a way through an organisational problem, creating opportunities for researchers or industrial partners, or taking a promising idea and making it concrete.

Today, I work at the University of Southampton as a Senior Enterprise Fellow, where much of my work is around research impact, innovation and engagement.

I still write code.
I still care about AI.
But I'm increasingly interested in the wider problem of how technology leaves the laboratory: who shapes it, who gets involved, what makes people actually use it and whether what we build ultimately makes a useful difference.
